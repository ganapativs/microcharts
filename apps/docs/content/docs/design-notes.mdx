---
title: Design notes
description: The reasoning behind microcharts' defaults — why there are no axes or legends, why areas anchor at zero, why color encodes and never decorates, and why every chart stays small.
---

microcharts has opinions, and they're baked into the defaults so you rarely have to think
about them. Here's the reasoning, in case you're curious.

## Direct labels beat chrome

At this size, an axis, a legend, and a gridline all cost more ink than they return. So there
are none. A value sits at the end of the line; a min and max mark themselves. The chart is
almost all data.

## Areas anchor at zero

A filled Sparkline always measures from zero, because an area that floats on an arbitrary
baseline exaggerates change. Length should mean magnitude, honestly.

## Color encodes, never decorates

There is one accent per context, and a color change always means a meaning change. Positive
and negative come from a color-blind-safe pair, and they're always doubled by a glyph or a
position so the chart survives without color at all.

## Small is a material, not a limit

Composition, contrast, and the auto-summary all have to survive the smallest supported size.
That constraint is what makes the charts calm: there's no room for anything that isn't
carrying information.

## What we don't ship

No pie, no gauge, no 3-D, no gradients-as-decoration, no drop shadows on data, no looping
animation. These aren't theme options you can switch on — they're designed out, because at a
glance they mislead more than they inform. Where a rejected chart answered a real question,
the catalog ships a faster replacement.
