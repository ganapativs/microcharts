QuantileDots answers "what are the odds?" — in **countable** form. It's a quantile dotplot:
each dot is an equal-probability quantile, so a dot is roughly a **1-in-N chance**, not a raw
observation. Add a `threshold` and the plot turns from shape into odds: the dots past the line
are re-inked and ringed, and the summary reads **"N in 20"** — because odds you can count beat
odds you must trust, and a frequency reads truer than a bare percentage.

## When to use it

- **Good for** — a "will we miss the SLA?" read in a sentence, odds you can count in a KPI card, a posterior communicated as frequency rather than percent.
- **Avoid for** — a precise distribution shape (HistogramStrip) or one estimate's interval (GradedBand).

## Why this default

20 dots, because odds you can count beat odds you must trust — a reader subitizes a handful of
highlighted dots faster than they parse "48%". Each dot is an equal-probability quantile, **not**
a raw observation, and the summary always frames the answer as a frequency ("10 in 20"), never a
bare percentage. Dots past the threshold are re-inked **and** ringed, so the count survives
grayscale, print, and forced-colors.

## Accessibility

The accessible name states the odds in frequency form — **"10 in 20 chances above 15 min."** —
or, without a threshold, "Most likely …; range …". The interactive entry is a probe: hovering or
arrowing moves a live threshold and the count past it recomputes as you go.
